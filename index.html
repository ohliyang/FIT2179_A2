Badminton Participation Across Australia (2024)
NSW and VIC contribute over 260,000 participants, while ACT records the highest rate
The hybrid map reveals geographic inequalities in badminton participation across Australia. NSW and Victoria dominate participant numbers nationally, while the ACT records the highest participation rate despite its smaller population. The proportional symbols emphasise participation count, whereas the choropleth layer highlights participation rate across states.
Key Insight: ACT has the highest participation rate, but NSW and VIC drive Australia's badminton participation by large number of participants.
Changes in Participation Rate by State (2023-2024)
Queensland recorded the largest increase, rising from 0.9% to 1.1%
The comet chart highlights uneven participation changes between 2023 and 2024 across Australian states. Queensland, NSW, NT, and WA experienced modest growth, while South Australia recorded the sharpest decline despite previously maintaining comparatively strong participation rates.
Key Insight: Participation growth was uneven nationally, with South Australia experiencing the most significant decline.
Growth of Badminton Clubs and Associations
Club numbers surged by over 20% between 2023 and 2024
The number of badminton clubs and associations remained relatively stable between 2020 and 2023 before rising sharply to 380 in 2024. This significant growth indicates improving accessibility, rising public interest, and the continued expansion of badminton infrastructure across the country.
Key Insight: The sharp rise in clubs during 2024 suggests rapid expansion of badminton infrastructure and accessibility.

Gender Participation Comparison
Male participation exceeds female participation by 40%
The isotype chart highlights a persistent gender participation gap, with male participation remaining consistently higher than female participation across both years. Despite this imbalance, female engagement levels remained stable, indicating sustained involvement among women nationally.
Key Insight: Male participation remains consistently higher than female participation, with the gender gap persisting across both years.
Participation by State and Gender
NSW and Victoria dominate participation across both genders
Male participation estimates exceeded female participation estimates across nearly every Australian state in both years. However, Queensland and Western Australia recorded noticeable increases in female participation during 2024, indicating improving gender representation in several major regions.
Key Insight: While males continue to dominate participation, female participation increased across several major states in 2024.
Participation Rate by Age and Gender
Teenagers recorded the highest participation rates nationally
Participation is strongly concentrated among younger Australians, particularly those aged 15-17 and 18-24. Female teenagers recorded the highest participation rate overall, while participation steadily declines across older age groups for both genders. This reveals badminton as a predominantly youth-oriented activity in Australia.
Key Insight: Teenagers aged 15-17 are the most actively engaged age group, with participation declining significantly with age.
Participation Rate by Household Income
Participation peaks among households earning $100k-$149k
Participation rates generally increase alongside household income until reaching the $100,000–$149,999 income bracket, where participation peaks nationally. Participation then declines slightly among the highest-income households, suggesting strongest engagement among middle-to-upper-middle income groups.
Key Insight: Participation peaks among middle-to-upper-middle income households rather than the highest earners.
Indigenous Participation Comparison
Indigenous representation fell from 9.9% to 5.0% in one year
The proportion of Indigenous badminton participants declined substantially between 2023 and 2024, while non-Indigenous representation increased correspondingly. This widening disparity may reflect changing accessibility, engagement opportunities, or participation barriers within organised badminton environments.
Key Insight: Indigenous representation in badminton participation declined noticeably between 2023 and 2024.
Participation by Employment Status
Casually employed participation declined sharply
Casual workers recorded the highest participation rate during 2023, although this declined in 2024. Meanwhile, unemployed Australians experienced increased participation rates, suggesting flexibility in time availability and lifestyle factors strongly influence badminton participation behaviour.
Key Insight: Participation among unemployed Australians increased in 2024, while most employed groups experienced slight declines.
WHERE: Places and Spaces
This section explores where Australians participate in badminton across urban, regional, organised, and informal environments
← Prev ↑ Top
Participation by Remoteness
Remote participation declined significantly from 2023 to 2024
Major cities consistently recorded the highest badminton participation rates across both years, reinforcing urban dominance in participation activity. Outer regional participation improved considerably in 2024, while remote participation declined sharply due to likely accessibility limitations.
Key Insight: Urban areas remain the dominant participation hubs, although outer regional participation showed notable improvement.
Where Australians Play Badminton
Informal and community-based participation dominates nationwide
Participation flows most strongly toward organised sports facilities, gyms, and leisure centres across Australia. Informal settings, including homes and public spaces, also contribute substantially, demonstrating the importance of both structured and casual participation environments nationally.
Key Insight: Organised sports clubs and gym facilities are the primary participation venues, especially in highly populated states.
Ways Australians Participate in Badminton
Nearly half of participation occurs informally with friends or family
Informal participation with friends and family accounts for nearly half of all badminton participation pathways nationally. While organised club involvement continues to play an important role, badminton's accessibility and social appeal mean that informal recreational participation is far more common throughout Australia.
Key Insight: Informal participation with friends and family is the most common pathway into badminton participation.

Financial Commitment to Participation
Over 215,000 Australians paid to participate in badminton
More than half of badminton participants paid to participate in the sport, contributing over $69.4 million in total expenditure nationally in 2024. Participants recorded a median expenditure of $100 and an average expenditure of $321, reflecting varying financial commitment levels.
Key Insight: Over half of badminton participants financially contribute to participation, generating substantial nationwide expenditure.
Badminton Injury Rates by Gender
Male injury rates remained consistently higher across both years
Male participants consistently recorded higher injury rates than female participants across both years. However, injury rates declined overall between 2023 and 2024, potentially reflecting improved safety awareness, better training practices, or enhanced participation conditions nationally.
Key Insight: Injury rates declined across both genders, though males remained more injury-prone than females.
Volunteer Contribution in Non-Playing Roles
Most badminton support roles are overwhelmingly volunteer-driven
The chart demonstrates that volunteering forms the backbone of badminton’s non-playing operations nationwide. Fundraising, administration, and support roles rely heavily on volunteers, highlighting the sport’s strong community-driven culture beyond active player participation.
Key Insight: Community volunteering is essential to sustaining badminton's non-playing operational roles nationwide.
💡
KEY TAKEAWAY
Badminton participation in Australia is shaped by strong urban concentration, youth engagement, and highly social forms of participation. While participation continues growing through expanding infrastructure and community support, disparities across geography, gender, and demographics reveal important opportunities for more inclusive development nationwide.
