Badminton Participation Across Australia (2024)
NSW and VIC contribute over 260,000 participants, while ACT records the highest rate
The hybrid map reveals clear geographic inequalities in badminton participation across Australia. Victoria and New South Wales dominate in participant numbers, together accounting for more than two-thirds of all participants nationally, while the Australian Capital Territory records the highest participation rate at 2.5% despite having a relatively small population, indicating a particularly active badminton community. Queensland and South Australia show comparatively lower participation rates despite their large geographic coverage. The proportional circles further highlight the dominance of populous states in overall participant numbers, while the choropleth layer exposes differences in participation intensity rather than raw volume.
Key Insight: ACT has the highest participation rate, but NSW and VIC drive Australia’s badminton participation by sheer participant volume.
Changes in Participation Rate by State (2023-2024)
Queensland recorded the largest increase, rising from 0.9% to 1.1%
The comet chart highlights uneven participation trends across Australian states between 2023 and 2024. Queensland, New South Wales, Northern Territory, and Western Australia recorded modest growth, whereas South Australia experienced the sharpest decline, falling from 1.9% to 1.0%. Despite small decreases, Victoria and the ACT remained among the nation’s strongest-performing regions.
Key Insight: Participation growth was uneven nationally, with South Australia experiencing the most significant decline.
Growth of Badminton Clubs and Associations
Club numbers surged by over 35% between 2023 and 2024
The number of badminton clubs and associations remained relatively stable between 2020 and 2023 before increasing sharply to 380 organisations in 2024. This substantial rise likely reflects growing public interest, expanding accessibility, and stronger institutional support following pandemic recovery. The trend suggests rapid grassroots expansion and improving national badminton infrastructure.
Key Insight: The sharp rise in clubs during 2024 suggests rapid expansion of badminton infrastructure and accessibility.

Gender Participation Comparison
Male participation exceeds female participation by 40%
The isotype chart highlights a persistent gender gap in badminton participation, with male participation consistently exceeding female participation across both years. Male participation increased slightly from 2.0% to 2.1%, while female participation remained stable at 1.5%. The repeated icon structure makes the disparity visually intuitive and immediately understandable for audiences.
Key Insight: Male participation remains consistently higher than female participation, with the gender gap persisting across both years.
Participation by State and Gender
NSW and Victoria dominate participation across both genders
Across nearly all states, male participation estimates exceeded female participation in both 2023 and 2024, with the largest differences occurring in New South Wales and Victoria. However, Queensland and Western Australia recorded meaningful increases in female participation between years. The comparison highlights regional differences in gender participation growth across Australia.
Key Insight: While males continue to dominate participation, female participation increased across several major states in 2024.
Participation Rate by Age and Gender
Teenagers recorded the highest participation rates nationally
Participation is heavily concentrated among younger Australians, particularly those aged 15–17 and 18–24. Female teenagers recorded the highest participation rate overall at 8.1%, while participation declines sharply across older age groups for both genders. The heatmap clearly positions badminton as a predominantly youth-oriented recreational and sporting activity nationwide.
Key Insight: Teenagers aged 15-17 are the most actively engaged age group, with participation declining significantly with age.
Participation Rate by Household Income
Participation peaks among households earning $100k-$149k
Participation rates generally increase alongside household income up to the $100,000-$149,999 income bracket, which records the highest participation rate at 2.0%. Beyond this threshold, participation begins to decline slightly among the highest-income households. This suggests badminton participation may be strongest among middle-to-upper-middle income groups rather than the wealthiest households.
Key Insight: Participation peaks among middle-to-upper-middle income households rather than the highest earners.
Indigenous Participation Comparison
Indigenous representation fell from 9.9% to 5.0% in one year
The proportion of Indigenous badminton participants decreased substantially from 9.92% in 2023 to 5.02% in 2024, while non-Indigenous participation increased correspondingly. This shift may indicate changing participation accessibility, engagement levels, or demographic representation within organised badminton activities. The visual contrast between years highlights a widening participation imbalance.
Key Insight: Indigenous representation in badminton participation declined noticeably between 2023 and 2024.
Participation by Employment Status
Casually employed participation declined sharply from 2023 to 2024
Casual workers recorded the highest participation rate in 2023, although this declined noticeably in 2024. Unemployed individuals experienced the strongest growth, increasing from 1.7% to 2.0%, while full-time and part-time employment groups declined slightly. These patterns suggest time flexibility and lifestyle factors strongly influence badminton participation behaviour.
Key Insight: Participation among unemployed Australians increased in 2024, while most employed groups experienced slight declines.

Participation by Remoteness
Remote participation declined significantly from 2023 to 2024
Major cities consistently recorded the highest participation rates at 2.2% across both years, reinforcing the concentration of badminton activity within urban Australia. Outer regional participation increased significantly from 0.6% to 1.0%, while remote participation declined sharply to 0.4% in 2024. Accessibility and sporting infrastructure likely contribute to these disparities.
Key Insight: Urban areas remain the dominant participation hubs, although outer regional participation showed notable improvement.
Where Australians Play Badminton
Informal and community-based participation dominates nationwide
Badminton participation flows most strongly toward organised sporting facilities, gyms, and leisure centres across Australia. Victoria and New South Wales contribute the largest participation volumes due to their population size, while informal settings such as homes and public spaces remain important participation venues. The diagram highlights the importance of accessible infrastructure nationwide.
Key Insight: Organised sports clubs and gym facilities are the primary participation venues, especially in highly populated states.
Ways Australians Participate in Badminton
Nearly half of participation occurs informally with friends or family
Informal participation with friends and family accounts for nearly half of all badminton participation pathways at 46.62%, substantially exceeding organised club participation. Individual informal play also represents a significant share, reinforcing badminton’s accessibility as a casual recreational activity. The distribution demonstrates the sport’s strong social and community-oriented participation culture.
Key Insight: Informal participation with friends and family is the most common pathway into badminton participation.

Financial Commitment to Participation
Over 215,000 Australians paid to participate in badminton
More than half of badminton participants (55.2%) paid to participate, contributing a total expenditure exceeding $69 million nationally. Although the median expenditure was relatively modest at $100, the mean expenditure of $321 indicates some participants spend substantially more on badminton-related activities. This demonstrates badminton's growing economic footprint within Australia's sport sector.
Key Insight: Badminton generates substantial economic contribution despite relatively low average participation costs.
Badminton Injury Rates by Gender
Male injury rates remained consistently higher across both years
Male participants consistently recorded higher injury rates than female participants in both years, although injury rates declined overall from 2023 to 2024. Male injury rates fell from 9.7% to 7.3%, while female injury rates decreased from 6.5% to 5.4%. These reductions may indicate improved safety awareness, training practices, or participation conditions.
Key Insight: Injury rates declined across both genders, though males remained more injury-prone than females.
Volunteer Contribution in Non-Playing Roles
Most badminton support roles are overwhelmingly volunteer-driven
Volunteering forms the backbone of non-playing involvement in badminton across Australia. Fundraising, administration, and general assistance roles were overwhelmingly volunteer-driven, while coaching and team management involved larger participation volumes overall. These findings emphasise the critical role community volunteers play in sustaining badminton operations nationwide.
Key Insight: Community volunteering is essential to sustaining badminton's non-playing operational roles nationwide.

KEY TAKEAWAY
Badminton participation in Australia is shaped by strong urban concentration, youth engagement, and highly social forms of participation. While participation continues growing through expanding infrastructure and community support, disparities across geography, gender, and demographics reveal important opportunities for more inclusive development nationwide.
